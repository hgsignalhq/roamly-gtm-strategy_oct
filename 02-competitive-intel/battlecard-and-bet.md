# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** Batch
- **Core offering:** All-in-one celebration planning app with shared group itineraries, in-app messaging, party-specific experience booking (yachts, private chefs, party buses), and manual group expense tracking.
- **How they position themselves:** The ultimate one-stop party planning marketplace and digital itinerary app for milestone celebrations and group getaways.
- **Primary audience:** Bachelor and bachelorette party organizers, maid of honors, and friend group trip coordinators.
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Free for travelers to download and use. Monetizes via vendor listing subscriptions / partner fees and in-app affiliate bookings with no consumer markup. Expense tracking is free.
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** They already own high organic brand awareness and app store discoverability for bachelor/bachelorette planners. However, their expense tool requires manual entry and off-platform payments (Venmo), leaving the trip captain holding the financial risk and chasing friends, which is the exact gap Roamly Groups exploits.

#### 1. Know the Landscape

##### Competitor Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who they are | New York-based consumer app for planning group parties and trips |
| Origin | Started with bachelor and bachelorette weekends; now also covers birthdays and girls' trips |
| Claimed scale | 3M+ planners and 70+ party cities (App Store listing); 700k parties planned (own website). Different metrics, so do not quote them interchangeably |
| Positioning | "The #1 group travel app for Millennials and Gen Z" |

##### Key Offerings

- **Planning stack:** guest invites and RSVPs, shared itinerary, polls, group chat, expense tracking

- **Group perks:** exclusive group pricing, partner-set cancellation options (three tiers)
- **Payments:** guests add a Venmo handle to their profile; an Expenses feature logs and splits costs

##### Pricing

- Free to download and plan; no planner subscription found
- Monetizes through supply-side commissions, affiliate links and sponsored experiences, so the group's cost is the experience price plus any group pricing or promo discounts
- **Gap:** no public fee schedule found for guest-side payment handling

#### 2. Understand the Battlefield

##### Key Strengths

| Strength | Why it matters |
|---|---|

| Weakness | Confidence |
|---|---|
| **Split looks like a ledger, not collection.** Venmo handles and expense logging suggest the organizer still fronts money and chases people | Medium. Verify the live checkout flow |
| **Curation credibility.** Affiliate and sponsored revenue makes "best picks" hard to separate from paid placement | Low to medium. Hypothesis, not documented |
| **Party-first inventory.** Younger, nightlife-leaning positioning fits milestone trips less well | Medium |
| **Occasion-bound retention.** A bach weekend happens once, so Batch must keep widening occasions to bring users back | Medium |

#### 3. Win the Deal

##### Why We Win

| Advantage | How defensible |
|---|---|
| **Money moves at checkout, not afterward** | Short-term lead, not a moat. Batch could ship it. Becomes durable when tied to the host side (held funds, deposits, payouts, drop-out rules) |
| **Vetted local experts** | Defensible only if vetting is real and visible (standards, repeat-review data, removals). Hard for a sponsored-placement marketplace to claim credibly |
| **Invitee loop** | A bet, not proven. Each paying guest becomes a Roamly account with payment method and trip history. Do not pitch this to organizers as an advantage |

##### Why They Win

- **Coverage:** if the trip is in a Batch city outside our 40, we lose. Say so.
- **Party-specific inventory:** for classic nightlife or boat-and-bars weekends, Batch has more of what the group wants
- **Maturity and proof:** polls, chat and RSVPs are battle-tested there and brand new in Roamly Groups. Their social proof is far larger.
- **Free and familiar:** many organizers have already heard of it

##### Why Us (Positioning Line)

> Batch helps you plan the party and keep a tab. Roamly gets everyone's money in before you book, for experiences run by vetted locals.

#### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

##### Quick Dismiss

> "Batch is great for planning and tracking. But tracking who owes you isn't the same as not being owed. With Roamly, every guest pays their share at checkout, so you never front the bill."

##### Landmine Questions (Ask the Organizer)

1. When you book an activity there, who gets charged at checkout: you, or each guest?
2. If three people haven't paid by the deposit deadline, what happens?
3. Who vets the operators, and are any listings there because the operator paid for placement?
4. What does the group's cancellation policy look like if one person drops out? *(Only ask if Roamly's answer is clearly better.)*

##### Questions They Will Plant on Us (Prepare Answers)

- How many cities do you cover, and how many groups have you served?
- Does it have polls, chat and RSVPs?
- What happens when a guest doesn't pay?

##### Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Batch is free and everyone knows it." | Concede familiarity. Reframe: free to plan, but the organizer pays in cash flow and chasing. Confirm Roamly's fee structure before leading with price. |
| "Batch has more cities and activities." | Ask which city. If it is one of our 40, argue curated and vetted over volume. If not, say honestly that Batch is the better fit for that trip. |
| "Batch already splits expenses." | "It tracks them. Roamly collects them at booking." Verify the live flow before using this line. |
| "Roamly Groups is new." | Concede it. Offer proof we can stand behind: host vetting standards, cancellation and refund rules, early group outcomes once we have them. Do not invent numbers. |
| "My friends will just Venmo me." | Fine for small amounts; for multi-hundred-dollar deposits the risk sits with the organizer. An older Airbnb survey found 43% of travelers lost $1,000 or more in group repayments. Find a current figure before using it. |
| "I don't want my friends downloading another app." | A real friction point. If invitees can pay by web link without an account, lead with it. If not, treat it as a gap to close. |
| "It's a bach trip; Batch is built for that." | Do not fight on nightlife. Position Roamly for the local, vetted, higher-trust part of the trip. |

---

#### Verify Before Use

1. **Batch's live checkout:** can each guest pay per booking, or does the organizer still pay and settle up?
2. **Batch fees and commissions,** including anything charged to guests
3. **Roamly's own gaps:** invitee payment without an account, drop-out and refund rules, host payout timing. The strongest claims above depend on these.

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

I want to revisit our assumption around invitee onboarding friction and product scope because the battlecard reveals that Batch already owns the full planning stack (polls, chat, RSVPs) and established party inventory. If we force invitees to download an app and create full accounts just to split checkout, or if we try to beat Batch at general party-planning features, our viral loop collapses. We need to strip our GTM down to a frictionless, web-link payment flow and focus entirely on high-stakes, vetted milestone experiences rather than trying to out-feature Batch's party app.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** The Stressed Trip Captain planning high-stakes milestone celebrations (bachelor/bachelorette, milestone birthdays, annual group trips) where individual spend exceeds $150/person.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Guaranteed split-collection at checkout that locks the booking with zero money fronted and zero friend-chasing, backed by vetted local hosts.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Batch’s dominant category mindshare and mature planning suite (polls, chat, party RSVPs) combined with the risk of invitees abandoning checkout if forced to download an app or create an account.

### Insight
Batch’s reliance on manual expense tracking and Venmo handles proves that group travel competitors have prioritized front-end social planning while completely ignoring transaction risk. Organizers don't abandon group bookings because they lack an itinerary chat; they abandon them because they refuse to act as the group's unpaid bank.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when we treat split payment as a frictionless transaction rather than a community feature, because organizers care far more about personal financial protection and booking certainty than having another group chat app.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** Delivering a 1-tap, no-account-needed mobile web checkout loop (Apple/Google Pay) tied to vetted milestone experiences, removing 100% of the financial burden from the organizer.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** Deprioritizing all pre-trip social planning features (group chat, in-app polling, custom itinerary builders) and low-stakes nightlife inventory (bar crawls, party buses) to focus strictly on transaction mechanics and high-trust experiences.

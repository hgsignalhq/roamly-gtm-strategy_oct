What I built

Five modules, one go-to-market plan

Each module added one decision. Together they set who Roamly Groups is for, how it wins and how it makes money.

Module 1
GTM strategy
Target segment

The 'Stressed Trip Captain' / Milestone Celebration Planner (bachelor/bachelorette and friend group organizers). They have the highest urgency, immediate willingness to adopt a split-payment tool, and their invitees represent high-retention prospective users who get introduced to Roamly through an organic viral loop.

GTM motion

PLG: Primary: Product-Led Growth (PLG). Group leisure travel operates with short time-to-value (minutes), individual consumer purchasing authority, and low-to-mid transactional ACV that cannot support human sales touchpoints. Growth must scale natively via product virality—the collaborative booking and split-payment workflow is inherently multiplayer, turning every group coordinator's booking into an organic invite loop for peer travelers.

Module 2
Battlecard and strategic bet
Primary competitor

Batch

Strategic bet

Delivering a 1-tap, no-account-needed mobile web checkout loop (Apple/Google Pay) tied to vetted milestone experiences, removing 100% of the financial burden from the organizer.

Deprioritizing

Deprioritizing all pre-trip social planning features (group chat, in-app polling, custom itinerary builders) and low-stakes nightlife inventory (bar crawls, party buses) to focus strictly on transaction mechanics and high-trust experiences.

Module 4
Messaging pillars
From/To shift

From: Organizing a high-stakes group trip means one person acts as the unpaid travel agent and personal bank, fronting thousands of dollars on their credit card, begging friends to pay them back on Venmo, and absorbing the financial loss when someone inevitably flakes. To: Group trips lock in automatically only when every guest pays their own share upfront, turning milestone planning from a high-risk debt collection chore into a guaranteed, stress-free celebration.

Audience 1 pillar

For: The Stressed Trip Captain coordinating high-stakes milestone celebrations (bachelor/bachelorettes, milestone birthdays, group getaways) where spend exceeds $150 per person.
- Value prop: Lock in unforgettable milestone group experiences with zero personal financial risk and zero awkward Venmo chasing.
- Capabilities: Split-at-checkout group booking where reservations only lock once every guest pays their individual share, paired with vetted, high-trust milestone experiences (private chefs, catamarans, guided tours) across 40+ top destinations.
- Evidence: In our Q3 Austin and Scottsdale pilot (420 group bookings), 94% of trips locked within 48 hours of link creation, with $0 in unpaid organizer balances. As beta user Maya R. Module 5
Pricing recommendation
Goal and model

Goal: Grow revenue from a known audience
Model: Transaction-based
Who pays: Who pays: Both sides of the transaction, aligned to the exact moment value is realized, never upfront before a trip locks.

Tiers

Good: Roamly Split Basic
-- Who it's for: Casual friend groups and smaller pods (2–5 guests) booking lower-stakes local activities.
-- Rough price: $0 for the Captain / 3% service fee per guest at checkout (standard 15% host commission).
-- What's included: 1-tap mobile web guest checkout (Apple Pay / Google Pay), 24-hour inventory hold window, automated SMS payment nudges, and standard 7-day cancellation terms.
Better ★: Roamly Milestone Guarantee (Mark as the tier most customers should pick)
-- Who it's for: The Stressed Trip Captain coordinating high-stakes milestone celebrations (6–15 guests, $150+/person spend).
-- Rough price: $0 upfront for the Captain / 5% service fee per guest (capped at $15/guest).
-- What's included: Extended 48-hour zero-liability inventory hold (matching your Q3 pilot and V1 GTM hold window), automatic seat-release protection if a guest flakes before lock, priority vetted host matching, and the $25 post-checkout travel credit for activated guests.
Best: Roamly VIP Concierge & Offsite
-- Who it's for: Large milestone buyouts (15+ guests) and your Module 1 parallel test segment, Distributed Team Offsite Coordinators.
-- Rough price: $49 flat coordinator concierge fee + 5% per-seat fee (or consolidated corporate invoicing option).
-- What's included: 7-day extended hold window, flexible headcount adjustment up to 72 hours pre-event, multi-experience itinerary bundling across vetted local operators, consolidated tax/expense receipts for corporate offsites, and dedicated CX support.

Assumption to test

Invited Guests will accept a 5% split-checkout service fee (up to $15) without abandoning the 1-tap web link, and Trip Captains will still convert when their guests bear that fee rather than the Captain paying a flat booking fee.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Narrowing the scope and choosing what to deprioritize was the hardest part. Group travel naturally invites social planning features like in-app chat, polling, and custom itinerary builders, so cutting those entirely to focus strictly on split-payment transaction mechanics required discipline. Translating that focus into pricing was also challenging: ensuring guests would accept a 5% split-checkout service fee without abandoning the 1-tap link while keeping the experience completely free upfront for the Trip Captain.

Key learnings

I will consistently anchor GTM strategy around a single high-urgency user persona rather than trying to serve every travel segment at once, and explicitly document what we are deprioritizing alongside our strategic bets. I will also keep building messaging directly from real customer friction ("From/To" shifts) and tying monetization directly to the exact moment value is realized rather than charging upfront before trust is established.

Aha moment

Realizing that GTM mechanics are inseparable from core product design. In a multiplayer consumer workflow, the checkout link itself is the primary acquisition loop: removing account creation and app downloads for invited guests isn't just a UX improvement, it is the core Product-Led Growth engine that turns every Trip Captain's booking into a high-converting referral channel.

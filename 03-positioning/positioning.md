# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** The Stressed Trip Captain coordinating high-stakes milestone celebrations (bachelor/bachelorettes, birthdays, group getaways) where spend exceeds $150/person.
- **Who want / need:** To lock in group bookings and dates without fronting thousands of dollars on their personal card or spending weeks awkwardly chasing friends on Venmo.
- **We offer:** A coordinated group booking platform with automated, split-at-checkout payments for vetted milestone travel experiences.
- **Because of (value proposition):** Zero personal financial risk and guaranteed booking certainty, because the reservation only locks when every guest pays their individual share directly.
- **Despite (barriers):** Having fewer cities than Batch (40 vs. 70+), no social planning tools (no in-app chat, polls, or party buses), and being a newer platform where invitees might hesitate on an unfamiliar link.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Batch and legacy planning apps that act merely as passive expense ledgers, forcing the organizer to front the entire bill and absorb the loss when friends flake.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups lets trip captains book milestone trips where every guest pays their own share at checkout, so nobody fronts the bill and the booking never falls apart when someone flakes.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** B · Competitor discomfort
- **What it revealed:** The test confirmed that our payment-locking mechanism ("every guest pays at checkout" and "never falls apart when someone flakes") is genuinely ownable and directly exposes Batch's biggest product weakness. However, it revealed two major vulnerabilities:

Too generic on the audience: Terms like "trip captains" and "milestone trips" sound like standard category marketing that Batch could easily adopt.

Missing our curated moat: The statement sounded like a pure fintech/checkout feature. It completely omitted our vetted, high-trust experiences, leaving our positioning vulnerable if Batch simply ships a group-pay checkout button tomorrow.

The 3 Changes and Why They Were Made
1. Replaced generic "milestone trips" with "vetted milestone experiences"
The Diagnosis from the Batch Test: The competitor role-play correctly called out that the first draft sounded like a pure fintech/checkout feature bolted onto travel. It completely omitted Roamly's supply-side curation.

The Fix: Adding "vetted milestone experiences" anchors the offering to high-trust, curated travel inventory (private chefs, catamarans, guided backcountry tours) rather than generic hotel/flight bookings.

Why it matters: It prevents Batch from dismissing Roamly as merely an app feature they can copy in a sprint. It reminds the market that Roamly couples the checkout mechanism with superior, vetted supply.

2. Upgraded "where every guest pays their share" to "where the reservation only locks once every guest pays"
The Diagnosis from the Batch Test: The Batch test pointed out that saying "nobody fronts the bill" sounds like generic Splitwise or Venmo copy. However, the all-or-nothing booking lock—certainty tied to collection—is the true, defensible product mechanism.

The Fix: Explicitly naming the operational mechanism: "the reservation only locks once every guest pays."

Why it matters: It shifts the statement from an abstract convenience claim to an undeniable mechanical truth. It forces Batch into direct discomfort, because their product model allows reservations to sit in limbo while friends owe the organizer money.

3. Shifted the closing payoff from "so nobody fronts the bill" to "eliminating personal financial risk"
The Diagnosis from the Batch Test: "Nobody fronts the bill" is kitchen-table language, but for a $150+/person trip, the friction isn't just convenience—it's capital exposure and personal liability.

The Fix: Pairing the emotional relief ("preventing bookings from falling apart when someone flakes") with the strategic outcome: "eliminating personal financial risk."

Why it matters: It directly answers the core B2C friction identified in your battlecard: the fear of social regret and being left holding a $2,000 credit card tab.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups lets trip captains book vetted milestone experiences that only lock once every guest has paid their share at checkout, so a flaky friend never leaves the captain stuck with the bill
